Obidiah is one of the shortest books in the Bible, but that does not mean it does not pack a punch. Initially, the reader might be led to think this has to do with a prophet condemning the Edomites (the descendents of Esau), however, the main message is for **anyone** who wishes and cooperates with the downfall of God's people.

Who are God's people? That is one of the most discussed and I do not plan to give a revolutionary answer, since it deserves a dedicated space. Nonetheless, in the context of this book, God's people is anyone who follows the **God of Abraham, Isaac and Jacob**.

The following notes summarize the whole book.

- **Ob 1: 1-4**: The Edomites pride is in full display in the opening verses of this book. The prophet calls them out, quoting their prideful comments like, "Who will bring me down to the ground?", to which the Lord declares He will do just that.

- **Ob 1: 5-9**: The Lord declares a series of misfortunes that will affect Edom, for example, treason from other would-be allies, backstabbing from those who wished peace on Edom, and people stealing everything Edom has gathered. 

- **Ob 1: 10-14**: Then comes the heart of this book's mission. The Lord calls out Edom's passive behavior to the fall of Israel and Judah, as if Edom were a collaborator of enemy nations. And if this were not enough, Edom was gloating over Israel/Judah's misfortunes.

- **Ob 1: 15-18**: The prophet warns Edom (and the rest of those who are reading this book) that the day of the Lord is near upon all nations. The Lord will execute vengence over those who wished or acted on Israel/Judah's downfall, stating, that what was done to his people, so shall be done to them. This part ends with the Lord declaring Israel and Judah rising up while Edom fall down.

- **Ob 1: 19-21**: Obidiah ends with the Lord declaring that the diaspora or the exiles of Israel and Judah will eventually possess "Mount Esau", thus the Lord reigning over it.
