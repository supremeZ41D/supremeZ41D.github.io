# Romans 1

- Paul wastes no time greeting the Romans, in which he declares the following from Jesus Christ **(Rom 1: 3-6)**:
  - God's Son was promised by the prophets,
  - A descendent of King David,
  - Someone who resurrected from the dead,
  - Named Lord,
  - Through whom we received grace and apostleship.

- The Gospel is the power of God for salvation. To the **Jew first** and then to the Greek (or anyone else, **Rom 1: 16**).

- The righteousness of God is revealed and live by in faith.

- Some people have known about God's attributes, however, these have been ignored or suppressed by them. They have no excuse **(Rom 1: 18-19)**.

- These people have used idolatry and they have submitted themselves to impurity **(Rom 1: 21-24)**.

- God gives them up to their passions, to their unnatural relations **(Rom1: 26-27)**.

- Paul provides of big list of sins, that those who know God but are unwilling to acknowledge Him, commit **(Rom 1: 28-32)**. 


# Romans 2

- Nonetheless, even when people who know of the God of Israel, commit such things, those who judge them and commit the same acts of sins will also receive the same judgement from God **(Rom 2: 1-5)**.

- Still in the context of people who know who the God of Israel is and what He stands for, God sees the works of man for judgement **(Rom 2: 6-11)**.

- Jew (those under the Law) and Gentile (those not under the Law) have no excuse for their sinful behavior, meaning, even for those who never knew of the Law, they will also see God's judgement because of their sinful acts **(Rom 2: 12)**.

- Now, for those who are not under the Law (Gentiles), who "_by nature do what the Law requires, they are a law to themselves..._". This means that for them, unknowingly, the law is written in their hearts **(Rom 2: 13-15)**.

- Paul calls out the hypocrisy of Jewish teachers of the Law, stating that they who rightfully teach it should also obey it **(Rom 2 :17-24)**.

- Paul puts circumcision into perspective. According to him, circumcision has no value to a Jew that does not obey the Law, whereas for the uncircumcised, keeping, "_the precepts of the Law_" will be regarded as having circumcision. This means, no matter if the person is Jew or Gentile (circumcised or not), keeping the Law goes above race and culture **(Rom 2: 25-27)**.

- To put emphasis on this last point, Paul writes the following in **Romans 2: 28-29**: "_For no one is a Jew who is merely one outwardly, nor is circumcision outward or physical. But a Jew is one inwardly, and circumcision is a matter of the heart, by the Spirit, not by the letter. His praise is not from man but from God._"

# Romans 3

- Paul answers a hypothetical or real question: "_Then what advantage has a Jew?_". To which he answers, "_Much in every way._". Jews were _entrusted_ with God's word, laws, wonders, etc. The unfaithfulness of some Jews will never nullify God's faithfulness **(Rom 3: 1-4)**.

- The notion of our unrighteousness serving to show the righteousness of God is refuted, along with why would God's wrath fall on to us if this were to happen. Unrighteousness will be condemned (**Rom 3: 5-8**).

- To emphasize on that last piece, Paul answers the question about whether Jews are better than everyone else, to which he answers, "_No, not at all._" Everyone is born under sin, and measuring any human's actions to the Law will always end up short; through the Law comes the knowledge of sin **(Rom 3: 9-20)**. 

- Since everyone will fall short of achieving what the Law wants us to obey, then righteousness of God is revealed through faith in Jesus Christ, "_whom God put forward as a propitiation by his blood, to be received by faith._" **(Rom 3: 21-26)**

- Now, Paul goes right at the concept of justification through routines and works, stating the revelation and manifestation of God's righteousness through faith in Jesus Christ eliminates boasting, meaning, people cannot justify themselves by what people do, rather by faith. Paul also expands the concept of God is not isolated to just to the Jews, but to everyone else **(Rom 3: 27-30)**.

- Finally, Paul answers the very important question: "Do we overthrow the law by faith?" No, we uphold it, as in, support it, defend it and maintain it **(Rom 3: 31)**.
